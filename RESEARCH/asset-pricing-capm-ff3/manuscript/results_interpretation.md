# Results Interpretation

## Story Spine

- Central claim: FF3 materially attenuates CAPM intercept magnitudes in the pinned 25-portfolio sample.
- Claim strength: descriptive in-sample benchmark comparison.
- Mainline: aggregate attenuation, weighting sensitivity, portfolio exceptions, then diagnostic limits.
- Non-goals: causal mechanism, universal model validity, trading advice, and joint model rejection.
- Evidence threshold: only deterministic outputs and reviewed source anchors support claims.

## Aggregate Finding

Across 756 months from July 1963 through June 2026, mean absolute monthly alpha for the value-weighted portfolios falls from 0.001979 under CAPM to 0.000871 under FF3, a 55.98% reduction. Mean adjusted R-squared rises from 0.7338 to 0.9096. This is consistent with SMB and HML absorbing return variation omitted by the market-only benchmark; it does not establish that FF3 is a structural truth.

## Sensitivity And Heterogeneity

The equal-weighted grid yields a similar 54.57% mean attenuation, supporting the prespecified weighting-sensitivity hypothesis. The result is not uniform: FF3 increases absolute alpha in five portfolio-weighting cases, and six FF3 intercepts under each weighting scheme cross the descriptive absolute-HAC-t threshold of 1.96.

## Diagnostic Boundary

Forty-eight of 100 fitted models trigger the Ljung-Box(6) screen and 45 trigger the Breusch-Pagan screen. HAC(6) therefore remains the stated covariance choice, while the frequency of flags prevents strong model-adequacy language. Shared size/book-to-market construction between factors and test assets is an additional rival explanation for the large fit gain.

## Implication

For this exact public-data vintage, FF3 is a substantially better descriptive benchmark than CAPM for average intercept magnitude across the selected portfolio grid. The evidence warrants a reproducible benchmark-comparison claim, not causal, predictive, or investment conclusions.
