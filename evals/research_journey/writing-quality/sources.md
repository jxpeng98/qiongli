## S2 — Assisted application trial

staff-assisted online application appointment or the usual written instructions.
The trial enrolled 480 applicants aged 65 and above. Its outcome was application
submission within 14 days of the appointment. A centre-adjusted analysis reports

confidence interval of 2 to 22 percentage points. The excerpt reports neither
benefit receipt nor independent use at a later visit. It does not report how
the centres recruited applicants or what training staff received.

## S3 — Follow-up survey

A separate Bellhaven survey followed 210 older applicants for three months.
Perceived procedural complexity was associated with a lower probability of

The survey excludes applicants who could not be contacted at follow-up.
It does not measure attendance at assisted appointments. Its self-reported
return-use outcome differs from the trial's recorded initial submission.

## S4 — Interview study

Interviews with 24 older Bellhaven applicants describe two ways of experiencing
assistance: some valued having a person explain an unclear eligibility question;
others described feeling unable to return without that person's help. These
are contrasting participant accounts, not prevalence estimates or demonstrated
causal mechanisms. The study does not follow later independent portal use.
In these notes, “assisted digital access” means a person helps with an online
procedure; it does not necessarily mean the applicant has acquired the skills
to complete that procedure alone.
