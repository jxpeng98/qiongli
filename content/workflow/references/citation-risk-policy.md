# Citation Risk Policy

Use this policy before final manuscript, proofread, submission, rebuttal, or presentation outputs.

## Risk Categories

- `missing_source`: claim has no source pointer.
- `source_not_in_bibliography`: ledger source ID is absent from `bibliography.bib`.
- `weak_support`: source is related but does not directly support the claim.
- `wrong_construct`: citation supports a different construct, population, or mechanism.
- `outdated_source`: claim depends on current facts but uses stale evidence.
- `review_as_primary`: review cited as primary empirical evidence.
- `citation_pile`: many citations are listed without synthesis.
- `invented_metadata`: bibliographic fields cannot be verified from project artifacts.

## Required Output

For formal project work, write `RESEARCH/[topic]/proofread/citation-risk-report.md`
or attach its sections to the selected review artifact through the existing write
owner. A direct passage draft/edit needs no report file; return only consequential
unresolved source gaps alongside the requested text when necessary.

## Coverage and placement

Check source-dependent assertions across the requested unit, including its
opening background and explanatory notes. Definitions, historical or institutional
accounts, prevalence/trend statements, field-wide generalizations and contested
assumptions need support appropriate to their specificity. Do not exempt a claim
because it merely introduces the topic. Reuse relevant foundational sources and
check current evidence when a claim depends on current conditions.

Place citations close enough to identify the supported clause or sentence. Split
a sentence containing differently supported claims when attribution is unclear;
a citation at the end of a paragraph does not necessarily support everything in
it. A source about the same topic is not sufficient: inspect the passage that
supports the particular claim, including scope and qualifications. Verified
bibliographic metadata alone does not verify its findings or justify a full-text
reading claim.

Use enough relevant sources to represent the evidence and consequential
disagreement, without padding a reference count or attaching every available
source to every sentence. The paper's stated aims, organizational signposts and
clearly identified original reasoning do not need invented external authority;
cite the evidence underlying an inference and distinguish that inference from
the cited author's claim. Preserve supplied citation keys and the requested style.
If a needed source is unavailable, mark the specific gap or narrow the wording;
do not silently substitute an adjacent citation or launch an out-of-scope search.

## Rules

- Cite primary empirical evidence for empirical claims.
- Cite reviews or meta-analyses for field state, not as substitutes for primary results.
- Cite theory sources for construct definitions and mechanism claims.
- Cite method sources for estimators, diagnostics, and reporting standards.
- Cite dataset and code sources when data or software provenance matters.
- Convert unverifiable source claims into gap notes rather than polishing them as facts.
