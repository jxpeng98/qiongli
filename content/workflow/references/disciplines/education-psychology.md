# Education, psychology and measurement

Use for learning, teaching, development, behavior, assessment, scales and mixed
methods. Reuse education/psychology profiles as method aids; choose procedures
for this design rather than treating profile thresholds as universal gates.

## Frame and search — A/B

- Name the construct, population, setting, age/grade, language and unit. Separate
  an intervention effect from score prediction, instrument validation or an
  account of experience. Achievement, engagement and satisfaction are not synonyms.
- Search construct synonyms, instrument names/versions and the learning or clinical
  context. ERIC and PsycINFO vocabulary can help when those sources are available;
  a candidate database is not a connected service or a completed search.
- Record which version/translation of a measure each study uses. Do not assume
  evidence for one language, age group or setting transfers to another.

## Common methods — C/I

| Work | Design and evidence to retain | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Classroom / school intervention | Assignment and analysis units, clusters, baseline measure, contamination, fidelity, attrition and estimand | Treating hundreds of pupils in a few schools as hundreds of randomized units; claiming gain in one group proves treatment effect |
| Pre/post or longitudinal study | Measurement occasions, baseline adjustment/growth estimand, dependence and missing follow-up | Universal ANCOVA-versus-change-score rules; choosing the specification after seeing significance |
| Scale development / EFA / CFA / IRT | Construct coverage, item provenance, response process, factor/item model, uncertainty, validation sample and intended use | Treating alpha, fit cutoffs or an N ≥ 200 rule as proof of validity; using one sample's exploratory fit as independent confirmation |
| Group / language / time comparison | Comparable construct meaning and measurement; invariance/DIF evidence appropriate to the comparison | Attributing a score difference to a group when measurement differs |
| Mediation / moderation | Temporal order, causal assumptions where claimed, interaction scale, uncertainty and sensitivity | Calling a bootstrapped cross-sectional indirect path a demonstrated causal mechanism |
| Learning analytics | Student/cohort/time split, target available at decision time, missingness, calibration and subgroup performance | Student leakage, future information or treating predictive features as intervention targets |
| Large-scale assessments | Dataset edition, sampling/replicate weights, plausible-value handling from its documentation | Treating one plausible value as an observed individual score or ignoring the sample design |

Power/precision depends on design, variance, clusters and the intended decision.
For fixed samples, report the limits or a justified sensitivity analysis. Low ICC
does not erase the assignment structure. Missingness assumptions require reasoning
and sensitivity; no observed-data test establishes MAR versus MNAR.

## Qualitative and mixed methods — C/E/F

For qualitative work, name the analytic tradition and keep sampling, researcher
position, evidence and interpretation consistent with it. Reflexive thematic
analysis does not require inter-rater reliability or a saturation count. A codebook
approach may use different procedures; explain the chosen approach.

For mixed methods, state why both strands are needed, their priority/sequence,
how their samples relate and where integration occurs. A joint display can connect
the quantitative result, qualitative evidence, convergence/divergence and combined
inference. Do not call separate results sections integration or explain away a
contradiction without evidence. A sequential explanatory design may sample cases
from quantitative results; a convergent design need not follow that sequence.

## Ethics and delivery — D/F/G/H

Address teacher/student or clinician/participant power relationships, voluntary
participation, applicable consent/assent, instrument permissions and re-identification
of small groups. Record the actual review decision; never infer permission from
an educational setting. Keep numerical effects, uncertainty, score units and the
population together in tables and discussion. Do not turn association into advice
that a classroom intervention will work.

Useful primary sources: [APA JARS](https://www.apa.org/pubs/journals/resources/apa-style-jars.html)
for quantitative/qualitative/mixed reporting; [WWC standards briefs](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/standardsbriefs)
for education-study attrition, confounding and baseline equivalence under that
framework; [IEA tools](https://www.iea.nl/data-tools/tools) and the selected dataset's
manual for complex assessments; [Braun and Clarke's FAQ](https://www.thematicanalysis.net/faqs/)
for reflexive thematic analysis. Apply the actual design and edition.
