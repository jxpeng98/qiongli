## Writing Harness Contract

This shared contract applies to Stage F workflows, skill cards and role prompts.
It specifies the result and boundaries; the active model chooses how to reach it.
Formal task outputs and Q1/Q2 gates above remain required for the selected task.
A direct paragraph answer does not require the full artifact set or a gate report.

### Trace the text being written

Use `references/evidence-verification.md` for manuscript-first claim detection
and source traversal during drafting and revision. Start from the actual text,
not only a completed reading note or a pre-existing claim map. Cover substantive
assertions throughout the requested unit, including background, methods, results,
discussion, captions and explanatory notes. Track independently supported clauses;
new explanations and connective inferences can introduce claims too.

Draft from inspected evidence with its scope and locator available. If a useful
explanation needs an uninspected premise, inspect the authorized source or keep
that premise as a named gap; do not draft it as fact and attach a nearby citation
afterward. Reconcile changed wording, attribution and manuscript location with
the final claim/source records. This is part of writing the selected unit, not a
requirement to start F4 or a separate reviewer for every chat paragraph.

### Result-to-claim decisions

Apply the three questions in `references/academic-output-rubric.md` to the claims
that determine the requested text. Inspect the current output/source and inherited
limits before reusing a draft or interpretation.

| What must be decided? | Evidence needed | What can proceed? |
|---|---|---|
| Is this a result we can report? | Executed output or inspected source excerpt, version, sample/denominator, units and uncertainty or qualitative context | Draft verified findings. An unexecuted analysis remains planned; inconsistent numbers block that numerical claim until reconciled, while unaffected text can proceed |
| Which wording does the evidence support? | C's claim type and assumptions, E/I's findings, source anchors and failed checks | State the strongest supported claim. Remove or narrow an unsupported causal/mechanistic assertion; adding "may" alone does not supply evidence |
| Can we interpret magnitude or practical importance? | Metric, scaling, time horizon, population and a justified benchmark or conversion inputs | Explain the available estimate and uncertainty. Missing context limits the practical translation; do not manufacture a benchmark or substitute an example number |
| How do contrary findings affect the argument? | Comparable results, negative cases, prespecified/exploratory status and source-specific limitations | Integrate supported convergence/divergence. An unexplained contradiction stays visible; choose narrower prose rather than inventing a mechanism |
| Is the requested text complete? | Its source-to-claim checks, applicable formal outputs and unresolved dependencies | Return the checked unit, or label the specific incomplete part. Separate an evidence-bounded draft from a gate pass or submission-readiness claim |

Carry the claim ID, source locator, supported wording and limiting assumption in
the existing interpretation artifact and claim-evidence records when those are in
scope. On a changed upstream result, identify dependent manuscript passages,
tables, abstracts and recommendations; revise them together within authorized
scope or name the remaining affected work in the handoff. Do not silently reuse
the old estimate or promote a previous review into evidence for the new claim.
Use **Re-review after a source change** in `references/evidence-verification.md`
to inspect indirect inferences and unmapped uses as well as recorded links.

### Direction and freedom

- Reuse relevant source material and settled decisions, including
  `context/boundary_review.md`, `context/decision_log.md`,
  `context/stage_handoff.md` and `review/self_critique_log.md` when present.
- Keep a coherent **Story Spine**: what the text argues, the section's job,
  evidence supporting its central claim and where that claim stops. Reuse an
  existing outline; a short edit needs no separate Story Spine presentation.
- Choose structure, drafting order, tools and chunk size from the requested
  length, evidence dependencies and risk. A bounded section may be drafted and
  checked as a whole. Split longer work when checkpoints help catch a specific
  risk, a dependency remains unsettled or the user requests staged delivery.
- Follow the user, venue or agreed protocol when it specifies a structure or
  review process. Do not infer extra approval steps from a template or role name.

### Evidence and quality

Shared evidence, meaning-preservation and authorization rules apply to every
writing card. Chapter structures, title formulas, clustering counts and analytical
lenses are optional aids unless the user, paper type or verified venue requirement
makes them necessary. Local venue profiles are discovery aids; distinguish sourced
requirements from editorial advice before using either as a completion gate.

- Check for mainline drift, unsupported claims, logic jumps, contradiction with
  settled decisions, and generic or vague claims. Preserve source meaning,
  numbers, citations, uncertainty and the distinction between findings,
  interpretation and implication. Never infer measurement from a study label.
- Match analytical depth to the section's job and evidence. Discussion should
  explain what the findings mean; methods and results may be descriptive.
  Mechanisms, tensions, alternatives and implications are useful lenses, not a
  per-paragraph quota. Narrow claims when evidence cannot support more depth.
- Interpret qualitative themes when supported, keeping quotations and episodes
  as evidence anchors. Do not invent mechanisms, negative cases or theoretical
  contributions to satisfy a writing template.
- Preserve the design's claim type from Stage A/C and the selected discipline
  guide. A predictor is not necessarily a cause, an indirect path is not a
  demonstrated mechanism, and a source interpretation is not a measured effect.
- Check numerical statements against the actual result: population, denominator,
  units, direction, time window and uncertainty. Distinguish statistical from
  substantive importance. A nonsignificant result does not establish equivalence;
  equivalence/noninferiority claims need their own design, margin and analysis.
- Preserve the source's level of methodological detail. A named analysis or
  transformation does not establish its unreported grouping, parameters or
  implementation. Check those qualifiers against the source before compressing
  prose; omit unsupported detail or label a necessary inference explicitly,
  rather than presenting a customary procedure as a reported fact.
- Separate planned from performed methods and confirmatory from exploratory
  analyses. Account for deviations, missing observations and null/contradictory
  findings before compressing results into an abstract or recommendation.
- For theory/humanities, make premises, source interpretation, objections and
  scope inspectable without forcing empirical section names. For mixed methods,
  explain what integration adds and where the strands disagree.

### Readable, specific prose

Apply readability while composing every requested section, including methods,
results and discussion, not only the literature review or a later J2 polish.
Use `references/scholarly-voice.md` for the shared clarity decisions and English
or Chinese phrasing. Keep a recognizable subject, clear referents and an information
order that lets each sentence develop the preceding point. Explain the actual
relation between ideas before adding a transition. Abstract nouns, stacked
modifiers and translated clause order must not obscure who does what or what a
finding means. Retain necessary technical terms and qualifications; neither
ornate phrasing nor uniformly short sentences establishes scholarly quality.

Make detail serve the argument: unpack a consequential concept, comparison or
inferential step with inspected evidence. Removing repeated generalities is
compatible with explaining the important point more fully. During revision,
preserve quotations, numbers, citation attribution and the author's intended
meaning; flag unresolved ambiguity rather than silently choosing a new claim.
When shortening, preserve the evidence-to-conclusion step. A compact sequence of
claims without their reasons remains shallow; replacing those reasons with an
abstract label does not repair it. Explain the relevant relation directly, with
the amount of detail the reader needs to assess it.

When a paragraph is shallow, locate the missing step: what the evidence actually
shows, how it bears on the paragraph's point, or what follows for this paper.
Explain that step with available source detail. Define a consequential term at
first use, unpack a comparison's basis, and explain how an inference follows
from its premises. A list of authors or a paraphrase of results cannot substitute
for the needed explanation. Unsupported mechanisms, extra citations and longer
sentences do not add depth.

Across paragraphs, carry forward a specific established point or unresolved
question. Check whether the next paragraph answers, qualifies, contrasts with or
builds on it; supply the missing reasoning or reorder within the authorized edit.
Do not manufacture continuity with “therefore”, “however” or a repeated topic label.
Keep consistent terms and explicit referents so that the reader can follow the
argument without consulting internal notes.

For a connected section or manuscript, use the existing Story Spine to track the
opening question, what prior work establishes, the paper's supported answer and
what that answer changes. Compare the introduction's promises with the actual
results and discussion; revise a promise or disclose an unanswered part when
evidence falls short. A contribution may clarify an established account's scope
or resolve a consequential comparison. Do not invent novelty to fill a gap slot.

Trace different kinds of reasoning to their actual basis. Reported source facts
need inspected source locations; this study's findings need its materials and
analysis outputs. An original synthesis or interpretation needs inspectable
premises and an explained inferential step, not an identical conclusion in a
cited paper. Distinguish that reasoning from the source author's conclusion and
from a hypothesis requiring new evidence. Reuse existing claim types and records;
do not create a second argument register or display internal maps by default.

### Literature review tied to the paper

For a manuscript's literature review/related work, reuse its research question,
central argument and scope before choosing themes. The section should explain
what existing evidence lets this paper assume, question or investigate. A missing
research question may permit a provisional source synthesis, but not invented
project positioning; ask for the missing focus when it determines the argument.
A section request does not by itself commission a systematic review or new search.

Develop the consequential comparisons beyond an author/topic inventory. Select
the details that explain the inference: what a source argues or finds, how its
evidence supports that statement, and what context or limitation changes its
relevance here. Compare studies or interpretations on a shared question using
their actual constructs, designs, texts, populations or time horizons as relevant.
Explain why convergence or disagreement matters; a difference in topic alone
does not establish a contradiction. Do not fill unreported methods or mechanisms
from expectations, treat overlapping samples as independent confirmation, or turn
abstract-only access into a claim of full-paper appraisal.

Connect the resulting synthesis to a concrete choice in this paper: a construct
definition, theoretical expectation, comparison, design decision or unresolved
question. "This is relevant to our topic" is insufficient without that connection.
Explain what remains unknown and what answering it would change. A list of
estimators, datasets or robustness checks does not by itself explain a substantive
contribution. Likewise, saying an estimate is not new or a dataset cannot identify
a mechanism does not replace explaining what the cited evidence establishes.
Keep competing explanations and limits that constrain the proposed contribution.
An unmeasured outcome is a gap in the inspected corpus, not proof that no prior
research exists. The evidence may support adopting or refining an existing account
rather than claiming novelty. These are analytical decisions, not a fixed
paragraph template, a study-count quota or a demand for a mechanism everywhere.

Use boundary reviews, feasibility decisions and analysis plans as drafting inputs,
not ready-made literature-review prose. Preserve consequential inferential limits
and methodological debates, but keep detailed implementation/reporting rules in
the section that needs them. The reader should understand the literature's actual
findings and unresolved question without reconstructing them from statements about
what this paper will not claim. A review may motivate a design; that motivation
must not crowd out the evidence it is supposed to synthesize.

When comparing estimates, explain whether their outcomes, populations, periods
and estimands permit the proposed comparison. A numerical ordering alone cannot
establish why studies differ. If the available sources do not support that
comparison, distinguish their questions and preserve the uncertainty rather than
inventing a reconciliation. For a supplied section, check available surrounding
definitions before treating an unexplained term as absent from the whole paper.

### Citation coverage and explanatory notes

Apply `references/citation-risk-policy.md` throughout the requested text,
including background, definitions and footnotes, not just the central claims.
Add inspected, claim-matching support where it is needed; citation coverage is
not a target number of references. Do not fabricate a source or a detail to make
a paragraph appear more complete. Keep unsupported assertions visibly unresolved
or narrow them to the available evidence.

Use an explanatory footnote/endnote when a useful qualification, term distinction,
historical aside or secondary detail would interrupt the main argument. Keep the
main claim, the evidence needed to assess it and consequential inferential limits
in the body. A note must not conceal a contradiction or missing support. Distinguish
explanatory notes from a venue's bibliographic note system: both may need source
citations, and neither exempts its factual claims from checking.

Make the body understandable on its own. A concept needed to follow the argument
belongs in the body; a secondary naming distinction or source-specific detail may
belong in a note. When such a detail matters for the intended reader, supply its
supported explanation instead of merely leaving a technical label unexplained.
Track a note's substantive assertions to their own passages, not automatically
to the nearest body citation. Do not invent extra content to force a footnote.

Follow the requested format and venue's note rules; use Markdown footnotes only
when the deliverable supports them. Preserve existing note identifiers where
possible, resolve every marker to its definition, avoid duplicate definitions
and check citations inside notes. If notes are forbidden, incorporate the useful
explanation concisely in the permitted form. Do not add notes without a reader
need, invent page locators, or use notes to evade the requested length limit.

### Review and completion

Read the complete requested unit for flow and depth: can the reader follow the
argument without reconstructing missing steps? For related work, inspect the
source comparisons and their specific connection to the paper. Check citation
coverage in background as well as analysis, and note placement and references
when notes are used. Revise the demonstrated defect; do not append these internal
checks as boilerplate to a requested passage.

Check the delivered unit against its opening question and carry the same
constructs, populations, outcomes and time frames through its later conclusions.
When the scope changes, explain that change. Check how each paragraph advances
the preceding point; a set of individually fluent paragraphs may still fail as a
section. Within the requested scope, repair a missing link or expose an unresolved
premise before spending effort on stylistic polish. Do not infer whole-manuscript
coherence from an isolated paragraph or silently reorganize an unrequested section.

Keep two review conclusions distinct: whether the statements are supported, and
whether the complete unit explains and connects them clearly. A source-accurate
author list can still fail depth/coherence; fluent prose can still overclaim.
After a meaning-bearing edit, recheck the affected claim and source location as
well as the revised flow. Do not mark the unit complete with a known missing
explanation, unsupported bridge or unresolved substantive note presented as fact.

Check the delivered text against the requested length and format, as well as its
claims. Use the user's counting rule. If none is specified, count words for an
English word limit; for a Chinese character limit, count non-whitespace Unicode
characters in the prose, including punctuation and digits, excluding standalone
source-anchor citations. Keep that convention consistent during drafting and
review. Citation strings, headings and editorial notes must not pad a short
paragraph. Check the actual final text rather than estimating from its appearance;
after editing, recount the changed unit. Return only the paragraph when that is
the requested format; an internal length check needs no extra checklist or count.
If evidence cannot support the requested length, identify the gap rather than
adding unsupported claims or silently returning an undersized completed draft.

Check the requested unit before returning it. Revise concrete defects and verify
what changed; further passes need new evidence, a remaining defect or an explicit
review requirement. Honor configured minimum passes and required independent
review, including settings carried by an existing run. A clean check is allowed
and does not require invented revisions. Missing evidence or an exhausted budget
does not establish readiness.

Continue within the agreed scope when evidence settles the decision. Ask only
for a consequential unresolved choice, scope change or required write approval;
continue independent supported work while that branch is blocked. Registered
project writes still use preview/approval/CAS and preserve user material.

Finish when the requested deliverable meets its applicable checks, or report the
specific unmet requirement. Record claim-support evidence and unresolved issues
at the artifact or claim level needed for traceability; routine paragraphs need
no checkpoint transcript. Preserve stable claim IDs and review issue lineage.

# Scholarly Voice: English and Chinese

Use while drafting Stage F prose and with J2 for stiff, translated or generic
scholarly prose. In revisions, resolve the specific expression problem; in new
drafts, establish clear information order from the start. Use readable, concise
language throughout the manuscript. Preserve evidence and logical meaning, then
choose the clearest wording appropriate to the reader. An academic register does
not justify obscure vocabulary or a harder-to-follow argument. This reference
does not require starting a separate J2 task.

## Shared editing decisions

- Keep names, numbers, quotations, equations, citations, terms, negation,
  quantifiers, scope and uncertainty. Suspected factual errors need a verification
  note; editing is not permission to correct the underlying research silently.
- Follow a supplied voice sample or venue rule without inventing personality,
  experience or a stronger position. Preserve required AI disclosure.
- Locate the point of each paragraph and the role of each sentence. Keep a
  stable topic and identifiable referents; use a paragraph break for a topic
  change when a supported transition cannot connect it naturally.
- Prefer a familiar word or direct verb when it expresses the same meaning.
  Retain necessary technical terms, define them where needed and use them
  consistently. Do not replace a precise term with a vague everyday synonym,
  or rotate synonyms merely to sound sophisticated.
- Cut repetition and empty framing, while retaining the premises, comparison,
  units and qualifications needed to understand the claim. A useful explanation
  may make a passage longer; disconnected short statements are not a successful
  simplification. Do not impose a sentence-length ceiling or readability score.
- Distinguish a genuine contrast from simple addition, and an observed sequence
  from causation. Retain helpful transitions; do not manufacture a missing premise
  with “therefore” or “因此”. Flag the missing reasoning instead.
- On the final continuous read, identify the actual claim, its reason and the
  link to the following point. If a reader must infer an unspecified actor,
  referent or premise, clarify it from inspected material or expose the gap.
  Polished vocabulary cannot make that unresolved step complete.
- Edit at the requested depth. A language pass preserves section order and
  fixed headings; structural editing needs a request that covers it. For coaching,
  show a few meaningful revisions and explain the decisions the author must make.
- Stop when the text meets the request and the source comparison passes. No
  detector target, banned-word list, required sentence pattern or forced slang.

## Language editing and meaning review

G4 checks tone and claim calibration against available evidence. J2 improves
expression while preserving the settled research meaning. Treat language editing
and meaning review as distinct tasks, even when one agent performs both:

- **Language editor:** read a coherent passage and relevant adjacent context;
  improve information order, idiom and sentence connections. Combine, split or
  reorder sentences within the requested paragraph or section scope. An edit
  may be longer when it restores a needed explanation. Remove a qualifier only
  when its deletion leaves the actual uncertainty, attribution and scope intact.
- **Meaning reviewer:** compare the original and edited versions for quantities,
  units, citations and their claim attachment, terminology, negation, quantifiers,
  population/time scope, causal strength and uncertainty. Report the exact
  changed span and its consequence, with a minimal repair when possible. Leave
  valid language choices intact; do not restart a general stylistic rewrite or
  add caveats merely because the prose is direct. No drift is a valid result.

Preserve uncertainty of meaning, not the number or wording of hedges. Observed
results and performed methods can be stated directly. Keep speculative mechanisms
provisional. A missing premise needs substantive review; neither a fluent bridge
nor an extra "may" supplies it. Necessary research limits remain where readers
need them, including independently read abstracts. Repeated boilerplate can be
removed when the local claim remains accurate. Tool failures, pending checks and
editor questions belong in the work record or a brief separate note, not inserted
as manuscript prose; never hide a material research limitation there.

For a requested cross-Host edit, hand over the selected manuscript version,
relevant context, existing terminology/claim constraints and any supplied voice
sample. Receive an edited candidate and unresolved questions. An optional
Codex → Antigravity → Codex sequence assigns editing to Antigravity and meaning
comparison to Codex; it does not select a model, launch a Host, transmit private
material or require multiple agents. Use the user's configured Host/model and
authorized handoff. A bounded edit can use two passages in chat with no new
project, ledger or mandatory handoff file.

Review the returned candidate against the same original version. If either
version changed meanwhile, compare the current versions before applying it.
For registered projects, preserve claim IDs and evidence links and use the
existing preview/approval/CAS owner to save the reviewed candidate. A language
comparison does not establish source support or close an existing evidence gap.
Stop once the requested language issues and actual meaning drift are resolved;
source/argument changes return to their substantive owner.

## English output

Use established collocations and keep the main subject and verb easy to follow.
Reduce noun chains and stacked modifiers without dropping qualifications. Keep
technical terms consistent instead of cycling through near-synonyms. Active or
passive voice and first person follow meaning and venue, not universal rules.
Preserve statistical “significant” and exact Methods procedures.

Carry a recognizable topic into the next sentence, then introduce the new point.
Clarify “this”, “it” or “they” only when the source establishes the referent.
Use sentence length to fit the idea. When translating, reconstruct English
clause order and articles rather than carrying over the source grammar.

Example (expression only):

> We conducted an examination of the association in 120 firms. The estimate was
> 0.18 (p = .03), but the design does not establish causality [12].

Can become:

> We examined the association in 120 firms. The estimate was 0.18 (p = .03), but
> the design does not establish causality [12].

The numbers, citation and causal limitation stay exact. “The effect proves…”
would change the research claim, regardless of how fluent it sounds.

Example (flow with consequential uncertainty):

> In relation to the difference in scores, the estimate was 2 points (95% CI:
> -1 to 5) [S1]. It is important to note that the interval includes zero.
> Equivalence is not something this analysis establishes.

Can become:

> The estimated score difference was 2 points (95% CI: -1 to 5) [S1]. The
> interval includes zero, and the analysis does not establish equivalence.

A meaning reviewer accepts this direct wording. "There was no difference"
or "The groups were equivalent" would change the conclusion and must be
flagged. Restore the affected conclusion, not the original awkward framing.

## 中文输出

按读者、学科和用途确定语体；简繁体随原稿或用户要求。普通学术说明应清楚平实，
正式论文保持学科所需的严谨程度；不把长句、抽象名词或公文化表达当作严谨。

先理顺段落意思，再调整信息顺序。能用具体动词时，减少“对……进行……”等空架子；
拆开堆叠的修饰语和“的”字链。主语可省略，但换话题时要明确指代。不要为了避免
重复而更换关键术语，也不要把所有长句切成短句。

“因此”须有原文支持的推论，“然而”须有真实转折。“此外”不能弥补话题脱节。
保留必要的连接词，不强制排比、四字短语或段末总结。处理中译英、英译中时，
按目标语言重组语序，不新增理由、例子或结论。

例如：

> 本研究对120家企业中的关联进行了检验，估计值为0.18（p = .03）。然而，
> 该研究设计不能确立因果关系[12]。

可改为：

> 本研究检验了120家企业中的关联，估计值为0.18（p = .03），但该研究设计不能
> 确立因果关系[12]。

“相关”不可润色成“导致”，“尚不能排除”不可简化成“没有”。完成后连贯通读，
再对照原稿核查研究含义、数据、引文和限制条件。

例如，已有样本结果支持正相关，待改的是重复限定和句间衔接：

> 在本样本的120家企业中，结果表明，X与Y或许可能存在正相关，估计值为
> 0.18（p = .03）[S2]。需要谨慎指出的是，该研究设计不能确立因果关系。

可改为：

> 在本样本的120家企业中，X与Y呈正相关，估计值为0.18（p = .03）[S2]。
> 该研究设计不能确立因果关系。

含义复核接受这个直接表述，无须恢复“或许可能”或“需要谨慎指出的是”。
“X提高了Y”改变了因果强度；“企业中普遍存在这一关系”扩大了范围，均需指出。
若原稿另有“这一关联可能反映资源差异，但该解释尚未检验”，其中的机制
不确定性必须保留，不能套用前例删成“这一关联反映资源差异”。

以上为合成教学样例；[S1]、[S2] 是样例标记，不是可用于论文的新引文。

# Academic Output Rubric

Use this rubric whenever a workflow or skill produces scholarly prose, evidence synthesis, code review, study design, or submission material.

## Three questions at consequential decisions

Use these questions when choosing a method, changing a central claim, resolving
conflicting evidence or resuming affected work. Reuse settled answers; routine
edits need no decision form or repeated interview.

1. **What must be decided now?** Name the requested deliverable and the affected
   RQ, claim or decision ID. Distinguish the intended claim from the claim the
   available design/evidence can support.
2. **What evidence would settle it?** Inspect the available source, result and
   protocol at their current revision. Separate observed facts, explicit
   assumptions and missing prerequisites. Ask only for information that could
   change this decision; a prior summary or review is not new source evidence.
3. **What can proceed, under what condition?** Choose a supported action, a
   bounded provisional draft, or a pause of the affected action. Name the missing
   input and the check that would permit that action to resume. Continue
   independent supported work. Missing optional context does not block the task;
   a missing required premise cannot become a passed gate.

The model chooses tools, sequence, methods and presentation within the authorized
scope. Stage C/E/F references supply concrete decision points. Preserve the
selected task's formal outputs, gates and protocol requirements.

Keep the decision, evidence basis and continuation condition in the existing
design, synthesis or interpretation artifact. For consequential project changes,
reuse `context/decision_log.md` and `context/stage_handoff.md`; preserve IDs,
source anchors, limitations and prior entries. Do not create a parallel decision
register. If a source/result changes, revisit only dependent decisions and claims,
and identify affected downstream artifacts before reusing an earlier conclusion.
Use the existing preview/approval/CAS owner for persistence; a candidate or
planned diagnostic is not an applied decision or completed check.

## Claim Discipline

- Bind each core claim to an evidence source, dataset result, or named project artifact.
- Separate `finding`, `interpretation`, and `implication`; do not let implications masquerade as results.
- Match claim strength and scope to evidence. State observations and performed
  methods directly; qualify uncertain interpretations or extrapolations where
  needed. Observational evidence can support a direct statement of association
  without supporting causation. Do not impose a hedge on every claim.
- Do not use empty intensifiers such as "important", "significant", or "robust" unless the text explains the criterion and evidence.

## Evidence Integrity

- Never invent citations, sample sizes, model outputs, statistical results, datasets, institutional requirements, or reviewer comments.
- If evidence is missing, write a gap note that names the missing input and the artifact that should supply it.
- Preserve uncertainty, contradictions, boundary conditions, and plausible rival explanations.
- Prefer field-specific terminology over generic template language.

## Scholar-Like Writing

- State a mechanism or theoretical explanation when evidence permits; otherwise report the supported pattern and its inferential limits. Label untested mechanisms as hypotheses.
- Name boundary conditions: population, setting, measurement choice, model assumption, time period, or scope limit.
- Include counterevidence or alternative explanations when they materially affect the claim.
- Keep prose direct and discipline-native; avoid generic AI phrases and repeated paragraph templates.
- Apply `references/scholarly-voice.md` to language edits and meaning review.
  Preserve consequential uncertainty without inserting audit commentary into
  manuscript prose or restarting style revision during a meaning comparison.

## Output Check

Before finalizing an artifact, verify:

- The artifact path matches the workflow contract.
- Claims are traceable to evidence or marked as hypotheses.
- Unsupported claims are removed or downgraded.
- Limitations are specific enough for a domain reviewer to evaluate.
- Missing information is disclosed as a gap rather than filled with fabricated detail.

# Invented evidence packet

Every named source, study, page and paragraph below is synthetic. Titles are
fixture labels, not actual publications or bibliographic records. All available
evidence is reproduced here; there are no underlying articles to retrieve.
Locator page/paragraph numbering is part of the fixture. Numeric confidence
intervals and uncertainty statements must be interpreted on their stated scale.
The two tasks use different study populations and materials.

## L1 — “Formative feedback in university writing: a synthesis”

**p. 4, ¶1.** This secondary meta-analysis included 18 randomized or
quasi-experimental comparisons with 2,140 novice university students. All compared
formative feedback with usual instruction or no additional feedback. The pooled
standardized mean difference for argument-writing quality was 0.31 (95% CI
0.16–0.46), with positive values favouring feedback. Quality was usually assessed
through claim clarity, relevance of evidence and treatment of counterarguments.
The synthesis reported substantial between-study heterogeneity (I² = 61%).

**p. 5, ¶1.** Fourteen comparisons assessed a revised version of the same essay;
four assessed a new writing task two to six weeks later. The subgroup estimates
were 0.38 (95% CI 0.22–0.54) for revisions and 0.12 (95% CI −0.08–0.32) for new
tasks. The interaction estimate and its uncertainty were not reported. Thus the
subgroups describe different observed results, but the report does not establish
that their effect sizes differ statistically. Eight comparisons had concerns
about allocation or outcome assessor masking. The authors did not pool outcomes
beyond six weeks or estimate effects specifically for evidence attribution.

**p. 6, ¶2.** The review categorized feedback as either directive comments
(proposing a specific change) or question-based comments (asking writers to
justify a choice). Many interventions combined both. The authors reported too
few adequately described comparisons to estimate a reliable difference between
these categories. Feedback delivery, practice time and revision opportunities
also varied. Individual studies below are separate synthetic records; L1 does
not state whether any of them contributed to its pooled estimate.

## L2 — “A revision cycle with explanation prompts”

**p. 7, ¶1.** In a required first-year course, 120 students were individually
randomized to usual instructor comments or the same comments plus a worksheet
asking them to explain which claim each cited finding supported and why a
revision was justified. Both groups received the same evidence packet, writing
time and one revision opportunity. Baseline argument scores were similar. All
120 completed the revision; 108 completed an independently written essay using a
new packet three weeks later, with six missing in each group. Assessors were
masked to allocation. The reported analysis used the available follow-up essays.

**p. 8, ¶1.** On the revised essay, the worksheet group scored 0.6 points higher
on a 0–10 argument-quality scale (95% CI 0.2–1.0). On the new essay, its advantage
was 0.5 points (95% CI 0.1–0.9). The rubric covered claim support and engagement
with conflicting evidence, but did not separately score citation accuracy.
The intervention combined explanation prompts with a written revision record;
the design did not separate their contributions. Students could consult their
old worksheets during revision but not during the new essay.

**p. 8, ¶2.** In interviews with 16 volunteers from the worksheet group, 11 said
the worksheet made them notice that a relevant citation could still fail to
justify the strength of a claim. These were retrospective perceptions; the
researchers did not directly measure change in understanding or test whether it
mediated writing improvement. Interview recruitment and the remaining five
students' accounts were not further described in the supplied passage.

## L3 — “Immediate automated comments and later writing”

**p. 3, ¶1.** A randomized trial assigned 90 novice students to an automated
comment tool or ordinary written rubric guidance. The tool offered immediate
sentence-level suggestions for clarity and evidence placement. Each group had
one 45-minute revision session. Tool users accepted an average of 72% of offered
suggestions; acceptance does not indicate whether suggestions were correct.
The excerpt gives no audit of suggestion accuracy. All students completed both
assessments and assessors were masked.

**p. 4, ¶1.** The tool group gained 0.9 more points on the revised draft's 0–10
rubric (95% CI 0.4–1.4). In a new essay two weeks later without the tool, the
between-group difference was 0.0 points (95% CI −0.4–0.4). This rubric emphasized
coherence and evidence placement, whereas L2 also assessed handling conflicting
evidence. Neither estimate measured time saved. The new essay result does not
exclude smaller beneficial or harmful effects and supplies no longer-term result.

## L4 — “Feedback uptake in an optional writing clinic”

**p. 10, ¶1.** This observational study followed 180 first-year students who
voluntarily attended a writing clinic. Instructors coded whether students
explained how they used feedback in a revision memo. Students providing such
explanations subsequently scored 0.7 points higher on a new 0–10 argument task
(95% CI 0.2–1.2), adjusting for baseline writing score and prior course grade.
There was no random assignment to explain feedback. Clinic attendance,
motivation and instructor contact could differ; motivation and contact were
not measured. The supplied passage does not report missing follow-up counts.

**p. 11, ¶2.** Students with lower baseline scores more often reported that
feedback phrases such as “develop the warrant” were hard to apply. No subgroup
effect estimate or comparison of accessible versus technical wording was
reported. These accounts suggest a usability issue, not an established advantage
of a particular vocabulary or feedback method.

## L5 — “What it means to use feedback”

**p. 2, ¶1.** This secondary conceptual explanation distinguishes correction of
a text from learning that can guide a later text. A novice may implement “move
this citation after the claim” without understanding whether the source supports
the claim. Independent argument writing requires choosing relevant evidence and
judging how far it supports a conclusion when a particular comment is absent.
A warrant is the reasoning that connects the evidence to that conclusion; it
need not be an explicitly labelled sentence.

**p. 3, ¶2.** The authors propose that explaining a revision can give students
practice in this judgment, provided they can interpret the feedback and have an
opportunity to act on it. They also note that explanations may merely repeat an
instructor's wording. This is a proposed account, not an experimental estimate
or proof that explanation causes transfer. The chapter reports no new participants,
effect sizes or tested mediation model.

## C1 — “实验提示与错误识别：研究综合”

**p. 3, ¶1.** 这项二次元分析纳入12项随机或准实验比较，共960名本科生。提示支持相对
常规说明在当次实验完成度上的合并标准化均差为0.35（95%置信区间0.18—0.52），
研究间异质性I²为54%。完成度通常按是否正确完成指定步骤评分，不等同于学生能
解释误差来源。其中仅3项研究考查无提示的新任务，合并标准化均差为0.09
（95%置信区间−0.15—0.33）。未报告两个结果类别的交互检验；不能仅根据一个
区间跨零、另一个不跨零就认定类别间差异已得到验证。

**p. 4, ¶2.** 研究综合中，提示内容、实验难度与评分标准不同，5项比较存在分组或评分
盲法方面的偏倚风险。作者未单独估计“先解释后看提示”的效果，也未汇总延迟超过
一个月的任务。C2及本研究是否被纳入此综合，材料没有说明。

## C2 — “提示顺序与操作完成”

**p. 6, ¶1.** 一项单校研究将64名学生随机分为先看提示组和先写操作理由再看提示组。
两组均可在练习中查阅提示，练习时间相同。后者在当次操作完成度0—10分量表上
高0.8分（95%置信区间0.2—1.4）。研究没有安排新的无提示任务，也没有考查理由
的准确性；材料未说明评分是否设盲。结果只反映这两种提示使用顺序在当次练习中
的差异。

## C3 — “为什么能完成步骤仍可能看不出误差”

**p. 2, ¶1.** 这篇二次概念性说明指出，步骤清单可以告诉学生下一步做什么，但识别
测量误差还要求判断测量值如何产生，以及某一步骤偏差如何影响读数。例如，知道
需要调零，并不自动意味着能识别未调零带来的系统偏移。“先解释”可能让这种关系
显现，也可能只是增加书写负担。该文是对学习过程的解释，没有新增实验证据，也
没有证明解释过程是任何已有干预的中介变量。

## Study — 本文研究记录（不是外部文献）

**§S1 — 设计。** 某校本科实验课10个教学小组按小组随机分配，两组条件各5个小组，
共200名学生（每种条件100名）。对照条件先看提示，干预条件先简述操作理由再看
相同提示。教师、提示内容、练习任务和练习时长一致。练习结束后两周，学生完成
新的测量任务，不能查阅提示。该任务的主要结果是独立识别预设测量误差的得分，
范围0—10分；不是实际仪器操作完成度。评分者不知组别。

**§S2 — 结果。** 干预条件新任务均分6.4分，对照条件5.6分；考虑小组聚类的组间
差异估计为0.8分（95%置信区间0.1—1.5）。两种条件各有8人缺席延迟任务，分析
基于其余184人；缺席原因未记录，未进行缺失数据敏感性分析。练习当次的操作
完成率分别为86%和88%；组间差异−2个百分点（95%置信区间−10—6个百分点），
不能据此认定两种条件等效。两项结果的量表和单位不同。

**§S3 — 解释所需的信息边界。** 本研究未评分学生书写理由的质量，也未直接测量
概念理解、认知负担或练习后额外学习。材料没有延迟任务前的误差识别基线得分。
本研究只包含一个学校与两周后的一个新任务；没有更长期结果。随机分组单位为
教学小组，不能描述为200名学生被逐人随机分组。置信区间已按小组聚类处理，
不得另编标准误、p值、效应量或亚组结果。
