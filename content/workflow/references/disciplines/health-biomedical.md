# Health, epidemiology and biomedical research

Use for clinical research, public health, epidemiology and laboratory studies.
This is research-method guidance; it does not establish clinical validity, ethics
approval, regulatory compliance or suitability for patient decisions.

## Question, evidence and design — A/B/C

Define the population, condition, intervention/exposure, comparator, outcome and
time horizon where relevant. Separate treatment effects, prevalence, diagnostic
accuracy, prognosis, prediction and mechanism questions. Use the applicable review
question framework rather than forcing every study into an intervention PICO.

For literature work, record database/query/date, vocabulary and free-text terms,
trial/registry identifiers and accessible report versions. Link protocols,
registrations, abstracts, follow-ups and primary reports to the same study; do not
count them as independent participants or silently replace a prespecified outcome.

| Study | Decisions and checks | Claim boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Randomized trial | Allocation/concealment, blinding where feasible, estimand, analysis population, intercurrent events, missing outcomes, harms | A protocol draft is not trial registration; per-protocol and intention-to-treat analyses answer different questions |
| Cohort / case-control / routine records | Eligibility, time zero, exposure ascertainment, outcome validation, confounders, censoring and competing events | Avoid immortal-time and selection bias; adjustment does not alone establish exchangeability |
| Diagnostic accuracy | Intended role, setting, index/reference tests, thresholds, blinding, verification and participant flow | Accuracy against a reference standard is not clinical utility; distinguish a diagnostic test from prediction-model development |
| Prediction model | Target use/time, predictors available then, development/validation separation, calibration, discrimination and uncertainty | Internal validation is not external validation or deployment readiness; leakage invalidates performance claims |
| Laboratory / animal / omics | Biological versus technical replicate, batch, randomization/blinding, assay quality, exclusions, multiplicity | Repeated measurements are not independent biological samples; discovery associations are not validated mechanisms |
| Health service / economic evaluation | Perspective, comparator, implementation, costs/outcomes, time horizon, assumptions and sensitivity | A model's cost-effectiveness depends on its context and inputs; do not generalize it without qualification |

## Analyze and synthesize — E/I

Retain outcome definitions, measurement times, denominator, effect scale and
uncertainty. Do not conflate odds with risks or hazards with cumulative risk.
Keep absolute and relative effects distinct; calculated absolute effects must
identify their baseline-risk source and time horizon.

Before pooling, check clinical/design compatibility and repeated cohorts,
multi-arm controls and multiple outcomes. Choose risk-of-bias assessment by study
design and certainty assessment by review question. Missing reporting is an
uncertainty, not proof that allocation, blinding or follow-up was inadequate.
Do not infer clinical benefit from statistical significance or surrogate outcomes
alone. Stage E owns the synthesis artifacts and limitations.

## Ethics and records — D

Keep consent/secondary-use basis, review body and actual approval status, access,
retention and sharing restrictions attached to the selected dataset/use. Removing
names does not establish anonymization of rare conditions, dates, images or linked
records. Do not send private records or unpublished participant data to an external
service without authority for that use. Unresolved permission blocks that action;
general planning from authorized material can continue.

## Reporting and delivery — F/G/H

Use `references/stage-G-compliance.md` to select the current standard and extension:
trial report versus protocol, observational study, diagnostic test, prediction
model, review, qualitative study or animal study. Map items to actual locations;
a completed checklist does not validate the design. Reconcile participant flow,
analysis populations, outcomes and adverse events across text, tables and registry.

Sources: [EQUATOR's study-type library](https://www.equator-network.org/library/)
for locating applicable standards; [CONSORT/SPIRIT statements](https://www.consort-spirit.org/published-statements)
for 2025 trial-report/protocol guidance; [PRISMA 2020](https://www.prisma-statement.org/prisma-2020)
for systematic-review reporting; [Cochrane Handbook, Chapter 10](https://www.cochrane.org/authors/handbooks-and-manuals/handbook/current/chapter-10)
for synthesis methods. These sources do not replace institutional decisions or
the specific protocol and current venue requirements.
