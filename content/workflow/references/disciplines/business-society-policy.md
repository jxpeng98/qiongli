# Business, society and policy

Use for management, strategy, marketing, organizations, sociology, public
administration, political science, political economy and geoeconomic statecraft.
Choose the question's level: person, team, firm, network, institution or state.

## Frame and find sources — A/B

- Distinguish a construct, a mechanism and a setting. A new country or industry
  alone does not establish a theoretical contribution; explain which existing
  claim it tests, extends or limits, supported by the literature actually read.
- Record theory definitions and competing operationalizations. Search adjacent
  fields when they use different names for the same mechanism; do not merge
  similarly named constructs without checking their definitions.
- For policy, distinguish enactment, implementation, enforcement and exposure.
  For sanctions/trade/security, distinguish announced measures, legal scope and
  observed compliance. Source publication date is not the event date.
- Combine academic sources with authorized primary records where needed: laws,
  policy documents, organizational records, surveys, speeches, interviews or
  archives. Label institutional statements and participant accounts by origin.

## Common methods — C/E/I

| Work | Minimum decision content | Main inferential risk |
|---|---|---|
| Organizational survey / SEM | Construct validity, respondent knowledge, timing, sampling frame, measurement model | Single-source covariance or a fitted path diagram cannot establish a mechanism or temporal causal ordering |
| Survey population estimate | Target population, probability/nonprobability sampling, strata/PSUs, weights and nonresponse | Weighting cannot by itself remove coverage or unmeasured selection bias |
| Comparative case study / process tracing | Case-selection logic, sequence of events, competing mechanisms, source access | Selecting only successful cases; treating a plausible narrative or repeated quotation as decisive causal evidence |
| Conjoint / survey experiment | Randomized attributes, prohibited combinations, outcome, target population, repeated tasks | Confusing an average marginal effect under the design with a universal preference or real-world behavioral effect |
| Text / network analysis | Document/network boundary, time, annotation, entity resolution and validation | Topics as unvalidated constructs; missing ties; inferring individual beliefs from aggregate text/network structure |
| Policy evaluation | Assignment, implementation intensity, outcomes, counterfactual and spillovers | Simultaneous reforms, changing denominators, endogenous adoption and post-treatment adjustment |
| Qualitative organization research | Analytic tradition, researcher access/position, case boundaries, memo/source trail | Forcing coding agreement, saturation or a preset data-structure diagram onto an incompatible tradition |

Use `references/disciplines/economics-finance-accounting.md` for the relevant
causal design only when it helps the selected task. For organizational multilevel
data, separate respondent count from the number of independent groups.

## Common content to organize

- Construct table: definition, theory source, level, indicator, respondent/source,
  time and rival meaning. Distinguish formative from reflective measurement when
  it affects the model; reliability does not establish construct validity.
- Case/event table: selection basis, source locator, actor account, observed event,
  researcher inference and unresolved contradiction. Negative cases need evidence,
  not invention to complete a template.
- Policy mechanism chain: instrument → implementation → exposure → response →
  outcome, with supporting evidence and a limit at each link. Trace distributional
  effects separately from aggregate averages.

## Ethics, writing and handoff — D/F/G/H

Protect employees, interviewees and politically exposed participants against
deductive identification. Permission from an organization does not necessarily
cover each participant or disclosure of identifiable quotes. Preserve actual
consent and access restrictions in the handoff.

Write results at the level supported by the data. State where institutional
conditions limit transfer. Separate participant explanation, analyst interpretation
and demonstrated mechanism. Practical recommendations need a supported link from
finding to action and its constraints; do not promise organizational improvement.

For method selection and reporting, consult [APA JARS](https://www.apa.org/pubs/journals/resources/apa-style-jars.html)
where relevant. For reflexive thematic analysis, the authors' [quality guidance](https://www.thematicanalysis.net/quality-in-ta/)
helps check coherence between the stated approach and actual analysis. Neither
source replaces the selected journal's or protocol's specific requirements.
