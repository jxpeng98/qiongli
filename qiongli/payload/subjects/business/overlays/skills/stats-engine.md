## Quality Bar

- The empirical design must match the business theory claim, unit of analysis, and data-generating process.
- Quantitative studies must document construct validity, source data, sample filters, fixed effects, clustering, and robustness.
- Qualitative or mixed-method studies must document sampling logic, coding reliability, triangulation, negative cases, and evidence chain.
- Strategy, organization, marketing, and operations claims must separate mechanism evidence from managerial implication.
- Every coefficient, quote, event, or case comparison must support a theory contribution rather than only describe a business phenomenon.

## Common Pitfalls

| Pitfall | Impact | Fix |
|---------|--------|-----|
| Treating a business case as theory | Produces a consulting-style paper without publishable contribution | Name the literature conversation, mechanism, and boundary conditions |
| Level-of-analysis mismatch | Individual, team, firm, or market claims are unsupported | Align constructs, data, and inference level |
| Convenience sampling | Reviewers doubt setting and contribution | Justify theoretical sampling or sample construction |
| Quote dumping | Qualitative evidence looks illustrative rather than analytic | Link quotes, codes, themes, and theoretical claims |
| Managerial implications before theory | Weakens academic contribution | Establish theory contribution before implications |
